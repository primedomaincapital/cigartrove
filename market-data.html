What premium cigars actually sell for

Published premium cigar import volumes, average prices by country of origin, and notable single-cigar prices from Cigar Aficionado's 2025 analysis of 620 blind-tasted cigars.

Premium cigar prices are set by origin, scarcity, and rating, not by a single index. Cuban cigars in the 2025 tastings averaged more than four times the price of Nicaraguan cigars, yet the year's top-rated value cigar — a 94-point Nicaraguan robusto — retailed for $7.25. Import volumes show the scale underneath those prices: more than 318 million premium cigars reached the United States in the first three quarters of 2025 alone. Prices below are suggested retail prices for cigars tasted in 2025, as reported by Cigar Aficionado; they are not auction hammer prices, and no secondary-market premium is included.

How to read these numbers

Included: United States premium import volumes by origin for the first three quarters of 2025, average suggested retail prices by origin across the 620 cigars blind-tasted in 2025, tasting scores and shares, and individual notable-cigar prices. Excluded: secondary-market resale premiums, auction hammer prices, and any paywalled price-guide table — no verified current auction-results series with realized prices was found, so none is shown. Country averages describe only the cigars tasted, not every cigar sold from that country; small samples (Costa Rica at 6 cigars, United States-made at 10) are flagged as such. Scores are blind-tasting results on a 100-point scale, not measures of resale value.
