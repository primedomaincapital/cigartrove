After a Decade in Court, the Premium Cigar Finally Has a Definition

What, exactly, is a premium cigar? The answer decided which products the FDA could regulate — and gave the trade a boundary line it had lacked since 2016.

Prime Domain Capital Insights · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

In a federal courtroom in Washington, a question that had consumed a decade of lawyers' time came down to something a tobacconist could have explained in a minute: what, exactly, is a premium cigar? The answer mattered far beyond semantics. It decided which products the Food and Drug Administration could regulate under its 2016 deeming rule, which products needed premarket approval, and which shelves could be cleared by a regulator's pen. When Judge Amit P. Mehta ruled, and when the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit upheld him, the trade got something it had lacked since 2016 — a boundary line.

The case, Cigar Association of America et al. v. U.S. Food and Drug Administration et al., was filed in 2016 by three trade groups: the Cigar Association of America, Cigar Rights of America, and the Premium Cigar Association. Their argument was that the FDA, when it deemed premium cigars subject to regulation, had failed to consider evidence that premium cigars are consumed differently from other tobacco products and by a different population. In July 2022, Mehta agreed, finding that the agency had violated the Administrative Procedure Act by glossing over comments submitted during rulemaking, including evidence cited by Cigar Rights of America from studies by authors affiliated with the FDA itself. In August 2023, the district court declined to send the rule back for correction without vacating it. The appeals court then upheld the vacatur, with Judge A. Raymond Randolph writing that the FDA's rationale was factually incorrect and that agency action resting on an unsupported factual premise is arbitrary and capricious.

The final ruling, issued in 2026, locked in an eight-point definition. A premium cigar must be handmade, wrapped in whole tobacco leaf, use a 100 percent leaf binder, contain at least 50 percent long-filler tobacco, and carry no characterizing flavor other than tobacco. Flavored cigars and most machine-made products fall outside the definition and remain subject to FDA rules. Cigar Association of America Chairman Javier Estades called the appellate outcome a great victory and noted that the association had led as named plaintiff alongside its partners. Cigar Association of America President Scott Pearce added the practical consequence for retailers: with the vacatur upheld, sellers would not have to worry about products being arbitrarily pulled from shelves.

That clarity arrives in a market that is large, concentrated, and exposed to forces no courtroom controls. United States premium imports reached 430 million in 2024, with Nicaragua accounting for 253.1 million, or 58.8 percent, the Dominican Republic for 106 million, and Honduras for 67.4 million. Concentration on that scale means a tariff, a harvest failure, or a political disruption in one country is not a footnote. It is a supply event. In March 2025, imports surged 29 percent year over year in a single month, following the announcement of new United States tariffs affecting all cigar-producing countries, and imports for the first three quarters of 2025 rose 4.6 percent to 318.6 million. Retailers who had read the tariff calendar stocked ahead of it. Those who had not paid more later. The lesson is not ideological. It is logistical: in a three-country supply chain, policy is inventory planning.

The counterargument to celebrating the court victory is that regulatory clarity is not the same as regulatory permanence. The FDA can still pursue premium cigars through formal notice-and-comment rulemaking, with a fuller record and stronger evidence. A future administration, a new study, or a shift in public-health politics could reopen the entire question. State and local rules — smoking bans, taxes, retail licensing, and flavor restrictions that operate independently of federal law — were never touched by this case at all. An operator who treats a court win as a permanent exemption is making the same error the FDA made in 2016: assuming a favorable record stays favorable on its own.

There is also a subtler risk inside the definition itself. A narrow, eight-point test protects the traditional handmade product, and it also draws a bright line that some producers will find themselves on the wrong side of. Blended products, novel formats, and anything with a characterizing flavor remain regulated, with premarket authorization, user fees, and marketing restrictions attached. Innovation at the edges of the category now carries a compliance cost that innovation at the center does not. Over time, that asymmetry shapes what gets made, not just what gets sold.

The original analysis that follows from all of this is that the cigar trade's next decade will be won on two disciplines at once: legal literacy and supply discipline. The shop owner who understands the eight-point definition can explain to a customer why one product sits in the humidor and another does not, without guessing. The owner who also tracks the import calendar — Nicaragua's share, the Dominican Republic's direction, the tariff schedule — can buy before a disruption rather than apologize after it. Either discipline alone is useful. Together, they are a moat, because most competitors will practice neither with any consistency.

For a club or subscription brand, the implication is concrete. Build the selection around products that clearly meet the premium definition, document why each one qualifies, and keep that documentation where staff can reach it. Diversify sourcing across at least two of the three producing countries so that no single disruption empties the humidor. And treat the court victory as a window, not a wall: use the period of clarity to build membership, reserves, and a reputation for straight dealing, because windows close and reputations, once built, are harder to take away than a regulation is to rewrite.
